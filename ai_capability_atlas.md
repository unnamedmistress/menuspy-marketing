# AI Capability Atlas

## Introduction: Why AI Capability Literacy Matters
AI capability literacy is essential for understanding how AI systems function, their limitations, and their potential. It empowers individuals to leverage AI effectively, ensuring ethical and informed usage across various domains. By mastering AI capabilities, users can enhance productivity, creativity, and problem-solving skills.

## Section 1: The 3 Core Domains Explained
1. **Knowledge & Learning**: Focuses on AI's ability to acquire, process, and apply information. Includes tasks like data analysis, pattern recognition, and continuous learning.
2. **Creativity & Content**: Covers AI's role in generating novel ideas, art, and content. Examples include text generation, image creation, and music composition.
3. **Analysis & Problem Solving**: Involves AI's capacity to analyze complex problems, optimize solutions, and make data-driven decisions.

## Section 2: The Skill Ladder (Dreyfus-aligned)
- **Novice**: Follows rules without context. Requires explicit instructions.
- **Competent**: Understands context and applies rules flexibly.
- **Proficient**: Intuitively grasps patterns and adapts strategies.

## Section 3: Cognitive Skills Hierarchy
1. **Basic**: Memorization and recall.
2. **Intermediate**: Application and analysis.
3. **Advanced**: Synthesis and evaluation.

## Section 4: The Capability Stack
- **Layer 1**: Foundational skills (e.g., data input/output).
- **Layer 2**: Intermediate skills (e.g., pattern recognition).
- **Layer 3**: Advanced skills (e.g., autonomous decision-making).

## Section 5: Dependency-Based Skill Map
Illustrates how skills build upon one another. For example, mastering data analysis (Layer 2) is a prerequisite for autonomous decision-making (Layer 3).

## Summary
AI capability literacy is a multi-faceted framework that combines domains, skill levels, and dependencies. Understanding this atlas enables effective AI utilization and continuous skill development.

## Glossary
1. **SPEC**: Standard Performance Evaluation Criteria.
2. **ZPD**: Zone of Proximal Development.
3. **Metacognition**: Awareness of one's own thought processes.
4. **Dreyfus Model**: A framework for skill acquisition.
5. **Cognitive Load**: Mental effort required for learning.
6. **Heuristic**: A problem-solving shortcut.
7. **Algorithm**: A step-by-step procedure.
8. **Pattern Recognition**: Identifying regularities in data.
